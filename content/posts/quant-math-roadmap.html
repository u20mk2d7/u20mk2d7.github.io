Fast-Track Quant Math Roadmap

A focused 4-month study plan to rebuild university-level math foundations and master the exact mathematical tools needed for quantitative finance.

1. Audit of Coursera Specializations (Keep vs. Pause/Drop)

Enrolling in six specializations at once (over 20 individual courses and ~500+ hours of material) scatters focus. To get into quantitative finance as fast as possible, cut what isn't immediately necessary and order the rest so each topic unlocks the next.

Tier 1: The Core Quant Math Engine (Must Do Now)

1. Algebra: Elementary to Advanced (Johns Hopkins) — ~25 hours total

Verdict: Keep & Finish First. A fast-track warm-up covering functions, polynomials, and equations so you don't get stuck on basic algebra later.

2. Mathematics for Machine Learning (Imperial College) — ~58 hours total

Verdict: Keep (Highest ROI for speed). Compresses the exact Linear Algebra (vectors, matrices, eigenvalues) and Multivariate Calculus (gradients, optimization) used in quant models and portfolio optimization. Course 3 covers PCA (Principal Component Analysis), used constantly to model yield curves and risk factors.

3. Foundations of Probability and Statistics (CU Boulder) — ~105 hours total

Verdict: Keep (The Heart of Quant). Quant finance is applied probability and stochastic processes. Course 2 (Discrete-Time Markov Chains and Monte Carlo Methods) and Course 3 (Statistical Estimation / MLE) are directly used for option pricing, risk simulation, and alpha signal testing.

Tier 2: Secondary / Selective (Do Only Specific Parts Later)

4. Data Structures and Algorithms (UCSD) — ~182 hours total

Verdict: Cherry-pick Courses 1 & 2 only. Skip Graph Theory, String Algorithms, and Genome Assembly (Courses 3–6). Only take Course 1 (Algorithmic Toolbox) and Course 2 (Data Structures) to write fast backtests and low-latency execution code.

5. Introduction to Discrete Mathematics for Computer Science (UCSD) — ~116 hours total

Verdict: Cherry-pick Course 2 only (or Drop). Most of this is pure CS theory. The only relevant part is Course 2 (Combinatorics and Probability), which is already covered more practically for finance in the CU Boulder Probability specialization.

Tier 3: Drop / Postpone Indefinitely

6. High-Performance and Parallel Computing (CU Boulder) — ~30 hours total

Verdict: Pause/Drop for now. Focuses on academic supercomputing tools like MPI and Slurm clusters, which distract from learning how to formulate and calculate quant models.

2. The Fast-Track Quant Learning Roadmap (4-Month Plan)

Do not take courses in parallel. Complete them strictly one at a time in this exact order:

Month 1: Reactivate Algebra & Start Linear Algebra

  • Weeks 1–2 — Algebra: Elementary to Advanced (Courses 1–3): Speed through familiar material at 1.5x–2x speed and focus on problem sets where you feel rusty (logarithms, exponentials, functions).
  • Weeks 3–4 — Mathematics for Machine Learning (Course 1: Linear Algebra): Focus on matrix multiplication, inverses, and eigenvalues (used in covariance matrices and portfolio variance).

Month 2: Multivariate Calculus & PCA

  • Weeks 5–6 — Mathematics for Machine Learning (Course 2: Multivariate Calculus): Focus on partial derivatives, chain rule, and gradient descent (used in calibrating pricing models and minimizing loss functions).
  • Weeks 7–8 — Mathematics for Machine Learning (Course 3: PCA): Focus on dimensionality reduction and covariance decomposition.

Month 3: Probability, Markov Chains & Monte Carlo

  • Weeks 9–10 — Foundations of Probability and Statistics (Course 1: Probability Foundations): Focus on random variables, expectation, variance, distributions, and the Central Limit Theorem (building blocks of asset return modeling).
  • Weeks 11–12 — Foundations of Probability and Statistics (Course 2: Discrete-Time Markov Chains & Monte Carlo): Focus on simulating random price paths (Monte Carlo) and state-transition probabilities.

Month 4: Statistical Estimation & Quant Coding

  • Weeks 13–14 — Foundations of Probability and Statistics (Course 3: Statistical Estimation): Focus on Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and confidence intervals (fitting models to market data and testing strategy significance).
  • Weeks 15–16 — Data Structures and Algorithms (Course 1: Algorithmic Toolbox & Course 2: Data Structures): Sharpen algorithmic thinking and execution speed.

3. Next Step After Month 4

Missing Piece: None of the Coursera specializations above cover Single-Variable Integral Calculus or Stochastic Calculus (Brownian Motion / Itô's Lemma), which are required for derivatives pricing (such as Black-Scholes).

Once you finish Month 4, read a practical quant textbook like Options, Futures, and Other Derivatives by John C. Hull or Paul Wilmott Introduces Quantitative Finance to connect this math directly to financial formulas.

4. Daily Action Checklist

  • Unenroll or hide High-Performance Computing and Discrete Mathematics from the active dashboard to eliminate distractions.
  • Complete Algebra: Elementary to Advanced (Course 1) within the first 7 days.
  • Maintain a dedicated markdown/math log translating every concept learned into how it applies to price data, returns, or risk.
