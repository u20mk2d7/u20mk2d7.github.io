What each slot works on

Work top to bottom. When an item is done, the next one takes its place. Nothing else starts.

08:00 weekdays: quant_proj

  1. fixed_point: write the failing tests, then the fixTests under asan-ubsan first.
  2. padding[34], OrderType::LimitMaker, MicrosTODO.md NOW items.
  3. Measure RTT to Binance (architecture §12 Q1)If p50 is above 20 ms, a Tokyo VPS moves up the list.
  4. Phase 3: ingress, book, capture writer runningStart capturing early so data builds up for Phase 4.
  5. Phase 4: replay and markout studyBuild the pipeline now; run the analysis after the estimation course.
  6. Go/no-go, regulatory re-check (§12 Q5), public write-up

09:00 weekdays and Saturday: math

  1. Algebra: Equations & Inequalitiesself-test firstGrade 21%. If the quizzes feel easy, tick it and move on.
  2. Algebra: Functions & Applicationsif neededFunctions, exponentials and logs: the entry ticket to calculus.
  3. Single-variable calculus: limits, derivatives, integrals, seriesfreeKhan Academy AP Calculus AB then BC, or MIT OCW 18.01. CU Boulder requires calculus through Calculus II.
  4. Mathematics for ML: Linear AlgebraCovariance, regression, PCA. Needed before the probability courses.
  5. Probability Foundations for Data Science and AI (CU Boulder 1)queuedAbout 43 hours. Starts after calculus and linear algebra.
  6. Statistical Estimation for Data Science and AI (CU Boulder 3)queuedAbout 29 hours. Confidence intervals are what the go/no-go needs. Taken before course 2.
  7. Discrete-Time Markov Chains and Monte Carlo Methods (CU Boulder 2)queuedAbout 33 hours. Monte Carlo is useful for simulating fills.
  8. Hypothesis testing: OpenIntro Statistics (free), inference chaptersThe CU Boulder series covers estimation, not testing. The go/no-go needs both.

Lunch, commutes and evenings: English

  1. Questions, Present Progressive and Future (grammar course 2)Monday evening. Word Forms is done. Then Simple Past.
  2. Essential Grammar in Use (Murphy), then English Grammar in UseOne unit per Wednesday evening, plus reading.
  3. Vocabulary with Anki: 10 new words a dayCommute out (17:00) and in short reviews. Use example sentences.
  4. Listening: BBC Learning English 6 Minute EnglishMorning commute and lunch. Read the transcript, then shadow out loud.
  5. Speaking: out loud at lunchA tutor or language partner once a week if possible.
  6. Writing: one paragraph each FridayGet it corrected, then rewrite it.
  7. Reading: graded readers first, then short news articles

Tuesday and Thursday evening: C++

  1. Modern C++ Features & Concurrency (Packt)Read alongside C++ Concurrency in Action (Williams). The course stops at C++17.
  2. Cache lines, false sharing, memory ordering, perf and flame graphsMeasure each stage of the quant_proj critical path in an optimised build.

Queued: starts only when a slot frees up

  1. Algorithmic Toolbox (UCSD)Weekend sessions, after Algebra course 1. Grade 19%. Assignments in C++.
  2. Python for Data Science, AI & Development (IBM)Needed before the probability courses.
  3. Data Analysis with Python (IBM)With Python for Data Analysis (McKinney). The pandas for tools/markout/.
  4. Interview practice, every two weeksLeetCode, Codeforces, CSES, and A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance Interviews (Zhou).
  5. High-Performance and Parallel ComputingStart after Modern C++ course 3.

Open decisions

  2. HKU route: paused. Choose between NEU then a master's and a PolyU SPEED top-up when you restart it.
  3. Investing: choose one topic after the job starts.

Reading list (when English reaches Stage 2)

  1. Trading and Exchanges (Harris), before the replay and markout study.
  2. Algorithmic and High-Frequency Trading (Cartea, Jaimungal, Penalva): market-making chapters.
  3. “The Probability of Backtest Overfitting” (Bailey et al.): before trusting any markout number.

Four rules

  1. End every quant_proj session by writing the next concrete step in TODO.md.
  2. If you miss a slot, skip it. Saturday morning is the only catch-up slot.
  3. The weekly review covers what finished, what moves up its queue, and TODO.md. It never adds new courses.
  4. One thing per slot. If the slot says English, it's English.
